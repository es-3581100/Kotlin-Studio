# Rauzy fractal

- [Ελληνικά](https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/%CE%A6%CF%81%CE%AC%CE%BA%CF%84%CE%B1%CE%BB_%CE%A1%CE%BF%CE%B6%CE%AF)
- [Français](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractale_de_Rauzy)
 [Edit links](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Special:EntityPage/Q3080267#sitelinks-wikipedia)                          From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia   Fractal set [![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3b/Rauzy_fractal.png/330px-Rauzy_fractal.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Rauzy_fractal.png)Rauzy fractal

In mathematics, the **Rauzy fractal** is a [fractal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractal) set associated with the Tribonacci [substitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_substitution)

     s ( 1 ) = 12 ,   s ( 2 ) = 13 ,   s ( 3 ) = 1  .   {\displaystyle s(1)=12,\ s(2)=13,\ s(3)=1\,.}

It was studied in 1981 by Gérard Rauzy,[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rauzy_fractal#cite_note-1) with the idea of generalizing the dynamic properties of the [Fibonacci morphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_word). That fractal set can be generalized to other maps over a 3-letter alphabet, generating other fractal sets with interesting properties, such as periodic [tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tessallation) of the plane and [self-similarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-similarity) in three [homothetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homothetic_transformation) parts.

## Definitions

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rauzy_fractal&action=edit&section=1)]

### Tribonacci word

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rauzy_fractal&action=edit&section=2)]

The **infinite tribonacci word** is a [word](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word_(mathematics)) constructed by iteratively applying the *Tribonacci* or *Rauzy map* :     s ( 1 ) = 12   {\displaystyle s(1)=12}  ,     s ( 2 ) = 13   {\displaystyle s(2)=13}  ,     s ( 3 ) = 1   {\displaystyle s(3)=1}  .[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rauzy_fractal#cite_note-ACOW525-2)[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rauzy_fractal#cite_note-PF232-3) It is an example of a [morphic word](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphic_word). Starting from 1, the Tribonacci words are:[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rauzy_fractal#cite_note-ACOW546-4)

- t  0   = 1   {\displaystyle t_{0}=1}
- t  1   = 12   {\displaystyle t_{1}=12}
- t  2   = 1213   {\displaystyle t_{2}=1213}
- t  3   = 1213121   {\displaystyle t_{3}=1213121}
- t  4   = 1213121121312   {\displaystyle t_{4}=1213121121312}

We can show that, for     n > 2   {\displaystyle n>2}  ,      t  n   =  t  n − 1    t  n − 2    t  n − 3     {\displaystyle t_{n}=t_{n-1}t_{n-2}t_{n-3}}  ; hence the name "[Tribonacci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribonacci_number)".

### Fractal construction

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rauzy_fractal&action=edit&section=3)] [![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/Rauzy_fractal_construction.png/330px-Rauzy_fractal_construction.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Rauzy_fractal_construction.png)Construction

Consider, now, the space      R  3     {\displaystyle R^{3}}   with cartesian coordinates (x,y,z). The **Rauzy fractal** is constructed this way:[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rauzy_fractal#cite_note-PF233-5)

1) Interpret the sequence of letters of the infinite Tribonacci word as a sequence of unitary [vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_(mathematics_and_physics)) of the space, with the following rules (1 = direction x, 2 = direction y, 3 = direction z).

2) Then, build a "stair" by tracing the points reached by this sequence of vectors (see figure). For example, the first points are:

- 1 ⇒ ( 1 , 0 , 0 )   {\displaystyle 1\Rightarrow (1,0,0)}
- 2 ⇒ ( 1 , 1 , 0 )   {\displaystyle 2\Rightarrow (1,1,0)}
- 1 ⇒ ( 2 , 1 , 0 )   {\displaystyle 1\Rightarrow (2,1,0)}
- 3 ⇒ ( 2 , 1 , 1 )   {\displaystyle 3\Rightarrow (2,1,1)}
- 1 ⇒ ( 3 , 1 , 1 )   {\displaystyle 1\Rightarrow (3,1,1)}

etc...Every point can be colored according to the corresponding letter, to stress the self-similarity property.

3) Then, project those points on the contracting plane (plane orthogonal to the main direction of propagation of the points, none of those projected points escape to infinity).

## Properties

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rauzy_fractal&action=edit&section=4)]
- Can be [tiled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tessellation) by three copies of itself, with area reduced by factors     k   {\displaystyle k}  ,      k  2     {\displaystyle k^{2}}   and      k  3     {\displaystyle k^{3}}   with     k   {\displaystyle k}   solution of      k  3   +  k  2   + k − 1 = 0   {\displaystyle k^{3}+k^{2}+k-1=0}  :     k =   1 3    (  − 1 −   2   17 + 3   33     3     +    17 + 3   33     3     )  = 0.543689...   {\displaystyle k={\frac {1}{3}}\left(-1-{\frac {2}{\sqrt[{3}]{17+3{\sqrt {33}}}}}+{\sqrt[{3}]{17+3{\sqrt {33}}}}\right)=0.543689...}  .
- Stable under exchanging pieces. We can obtain the same set by exchanging the place of the pieces.
- [Connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_space) and simply connected. Has no hole.
- Tiles the plane periodically, by translation.
- The matrix of the Tribonacci map has      x  3   −  x  2   − x − 1   {\displaystyle x^{3}-x^{2}-x-1}   as its [characteristic polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_polynomial). Its eigenvalues are a real number     β = 1.839286...   {\displaystyle \beta =1.839286...}  , called the [Tribonacci constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribonacci_number), a [Pisot number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisot_number), and two complex conjugates     α   {\displaystyle \alpha }   and        α ¯      {\displaystyle {\bar {\alpha }}}   with     α    α ¯    = 1  /  β   {\displaystyle \alpha {\bar {\alpha }}=1/\beta }  .
- Its boundary is fractal, and the [Hausdorff dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hausdorff_dimension) of this boundary equals 1.093364... (sequence [A272408](https://oeis.org/A272408) in the [OEIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On-Line_Encyclopedia_of_Integer_Sequences)), the solution of     2  |  α   |   3 s   +  |  α   |   4 s   = 1   {\displaystyle 2|\alpha |^{3s}+|\alpha |^{4s}=1}  .[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rauzy_fractal#cite_note-6)

## Variants and generalization

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rauzy_fractal&action=edit&section=5)]

For any unimodular substitution of Pisot type, which verifies a coincidence condition (apparently always verified), one can construct a similar set called "Rauzy fractal of the map". They all display [self-similarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-similarity) and generate, for the examples below, a periodic tiling of the plane.

Rauzy fractal

# Rauzy fractal

- [Ελληνικά](https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/%CE%A6%CF%81%CE%AC%CE%BA%CF%84%CE%B1%CE%BB_%CE%A1%CE%BF%CE%B6%CE%AF)
- [Français](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractale_de_Rauzy)
 [Edit links](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Special:EntityPage/Q3080267#sitelinks-wikipedia)                          From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia   Fractal set [![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3b/Rauzy_fractal.png/330px-Rauzy_fractal.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Rauzy_fractal.png)Rauzy fractal

In mathematics, the **Rauzy fractal** is a [fractal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractal) set associated with the Tribonacci [substitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_substitution)

     s ( 1 ) = 12 ,   s ( 2 ) = 13 ,   s ( 3 ) = 1  .   {\displaystyle s(1)=12,\ s(2)=13,\ s(3)=1\,.}

It was studied in 1981 by Gérard Rauzy,[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rauzy_fractal#cite_note-1) with the idea of generalizing the dynamic properties of the [Fibonacci morphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_word). That fractal set can be generalized to other maps over a 3-letter alphabet, generating other fractal sets with interesting properties, such as periodic [tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tessallation) of the plane and [self-similarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-similarity) in three [homothetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homothetic_transformation) parts.

Tribonacci word

# Tribonacci word

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rauzy_fractal&action=edit&section=2)]

The **infinite tribonacci word** is a [word](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word_(mathematics)) constructed by iteratively applying the *Tribonacci* or *Rauzy map* :     s ( 1 ) = 12   {\displaystyle s(1)=12}  ,     s ( 2 ) = 13   {\displaystyle s(2)=13}  ,     s ( 3 ) = 1   {\displaystyle s(3)=1}  .[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rauzy_fractal#cite_note-ACOW525-2)[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rauzy_fractal#cite_note-PF232-3) It is an example of a [morphic word](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphic_word). Starting from 1, the Tribonacci words are:[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rauzy_fractal#cite_note-ACOW546-4)

- t  0   = 1   {\displaystyle t_{0}=1}
- t  1   = 12   {\displaystyle t_{1}=12}
- t  2   = 1213   {\displaystyle t_{2}=1213}
- t  3   = 1213121   {\displaystyle t_{3}=1213121}
- t  4   = 1213121121312   {\displaystyle t_{4}=1213121121312}

We can show that, for     n > 2   {\displaystyle n>2}  ,      t  n   =  t  n − 1    t  n − 2    t  n − 3     {\displaystyle t_{n}=t_{n-1}t_{n-2}t_{n-3}}  ; hence the name "[Tribonacci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribonacci_number)".

Fractal construction

# Fractal construction

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rauzy_fractal&action=edit&section=3)] [![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/Rauzy_fractal_construction.png/330px-Rauzy_fractal_construction.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Rauzy_fractal_construction.png)Construction

Consider, now, the space      R  3     {\displaystyle R^{3}}   with cartesian coordinates (x,y,z). The **Rauzy fractal** is constructed this way:[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rauzy_fractal#cite_note-PF233-5)

1) Interpret the sequence of letters of the infinite Tribonacci word as a sequence of unitary [vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_(mathematics_and_physics)) of the space, with the following rules (1 = direction x, 2 = direction y, 3 = direction z).

2) Then, build a "stair" by tracing the points reached by this sequence of vectors (see figure). For example, the first points are:

- 1 ⇒ ( 1 , 0 , 0 )   {\displaystyle 1\Rightarrow (1,0,0)}
- 2 ⇒ ( 1 , 1 , 0 )   {\displaystyle 2\Rightarrow (1,1,0)}
- 1 ⇒ ( 2 , 1 , 0 )   {\displaystyle 1\Rightarrow (2,1,0)}
- 3 ⇒ ( 2 , 1 , 1 )   {\displaystyle 3\Rightarrow (2,1,1)}
- 1 ⇒ ( 3 , 1 , 1 )   {\displaystyle 1\Rightarrow (3,1,1)}

etc...Every point can be colored according to the corresponding letter, to stress the self-similarity property.

3) Then, project those points on the contracting plane (plane orthogonal to the main direction of propagation of the points, none of those projected points escape to infinity).

Properties

# Properties

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rauzy_fractal&action=edit&section=4)]
- Can be [tiled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tessellation) by three copies of itself, with area reduced by factors     k   {\displaystyle k}  ,      k  2     {\displaystyle k^{2}}   and      k  3     {\displaystyle k^{3}}   with     k   {\displaystyle k}   solution of      k  3   +  k  2   + k − 1 = 0   {\displaystyle k^{3}+k^{2}+k-1=0}  :     k =   1 3    (  − 1 −   2   17 + 3   33     3     +    17 + 3   33     3     )  = 0.543689...   {\displaystyle k={\frac {1}{3}}\left(-1-{\frac {2}{\sqrt[{3}]{17+3{\sqrt {33}}}}}+{\sqrt[{3}]{17+3{\sqrt {33}}}}\right)=0.543689...}  .
- Stable under exchanging pieces. We can obtain the same set by exchanging the place of the pieces.
- [Connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connected_space) and simply connected. Has no hole.
- Tiles the plane periodically, by translation.
- The matrix of the Tribonacci map has      x  3   −  x  2   − x − 1   {\displaystyle x^{3}-x^{2}-x-1}   as its [characteristic polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_polynomial). Its eigenvalues are a real number     β = 1.839286...   {\displaystyle \beta =1.839286...}  , called the [Tribonacci constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribonacci_number), a [Pisot number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisot_number), and two complex conjugates     α   {\displaystyle \alpha }   and        α ¯      {\displaystyle {\bar {\alpha }}}   with     α    α ¯    = 1  /  β   {\displaystyle \alpha {\bar {\alpha }}=1/\beta }  .
- Its boundary is fractal, and the [Hausdorff dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hausdorff_dimension) of this boundary equals 1.093364... (sequence [A272408](https://oeis.org/A272408) in the [OEIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On-Line_Encyclopedia_of_Integer_Sequences)), the solution of     2  |  α   |   3 s   +  |  α   |   4 s   = 1   {\displaystyle 2|\alpha |^{3s}+|\alpha |^{4s}=1}  .[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rauzy_fractal#cite_note-6)

Variants and generalization

# Variants and generalization

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Rauzy_fractal&action=edit&section=5)]

For any unimodular substitution of Pisot type, which verifies a coincidence condition (apparently always verified), one can construct a similar set called "Rauzy fractal of the map". They all display [self-similarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-similarity) and generate, for the examples below, a periodic tiling of the plane.
