In mathematics, the **tribonacci ratio** is a geometrical [proportion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspect_ratio), given by the unique real [solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_root) of the equation *x*3 = *x*2 + *x* + 1. Its decimal expansion begins with 1.839286755214161...  (sequence [A058265](https://oeis.org/A058265) in the [OEIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On-Line_Encyclopedia_of_Integer_Sequences)).

The moniker *tribonacci* was introduced by high-school student Mark Feinberg in an article published in the *[Fibonacci Quarterly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_Quarterly)* of october 1963.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribonacci_ratio#cite_note-2)

## Tribonacci sequence

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Tribonacci_ratio&action=edit&section=3)]

The first muddled mention of the tribonacci sequence is in [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin)'s *[On the Origin of Species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Origin_of_Species)* (1859), illustrating the population growth of elephants on the supposition that during their lifetime each pair of parents produces three pair of young.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribonacci_ratio#cite_note-13)

The number of [compositions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composition_(combinatorics)) of *n* − 2 into parts 1, 2 and 3 is counted by the *n*th tribonacci number (*n* > 1).

The tribonacci sequence is defined by the third-order [recurrence relation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recurrence_relation)      T  n   =  T  n − 1   +  T  n − 2   +  T  n − 3     for   n > 2 ,   {\displaystyle T_{n}=T_{n-1}+T_{n-2}+T_{n-3}{\text{ for }}n>2,}   with initial values      T  0   =  T  1   = 0 ,  T  2   = 1.   {\displaystyle T_{0}=T_{1}=0,T_{2}=1.}

The first few terms are 0, 0, 1, 1, 2, 4, 7, 13, 24, 44, 81, 149, 274, 504, 927,... (sequence [A000073](https://oeis.org/A000073) in the [OEIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On-Line_Encyclopedia_of_Integer_Sequences)).
 The limit ratio between consecutive terms is the tribonacci constant:     lim  n → ∞    T  n + 1    /   T  n   = τ .   {\displaystyle \lim _{n\rightarrow \infty }T_{n+1}/T_{n}=\tau .}
 An alternative relation for ⁠    n > 3   {\displaystyle n>3}  ⁠ is given by      T  n   = 2  T  n − 1   −  T  n − 4   .   {\displaystyle T_{n}=2T_{n-1}-T_{n-4}.}

The sequence is extended to negative indices using      T  n   =  T  n + 3   −  T  n + 2   −  T  n + 1   ,   {\displaystyle T_{n}=T_{n+3}-T_{n+2}-T_{n+1},}   obtaining (0), 1,−1, 0, 2,−3, 1, 4,−8, 5, 7,−20, 18, 9,−47,... (sequence [A057597](https://oeis.org/A057597) in the [OEIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On-Line_Encyclopedia_of_Integer_Sequences)).

The relationship between the negative and positive indexed segments of the sequence is given by      T  1 − n   =  T  n   2   −  T  n + 1    T  n − 1   .   {\displaystyle T_{1-n}=T_{n}^{2}-T_{n+1}T_{n-1}.}   This [reflection formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflection_formula) holds for all integers ⁠    n   {\displaystyle n}  ⁠ and can be derived from Agronomof's identity [[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribonacci_ratio#cite_note-14)[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribonacci_ratio#cite_note-15)      T  n + k   =  T  k + 1    T  n + 1   +  (   T  k   +  T  k − 1    )   T  n   +  T  k    T  n − 1   .   {\displaystyle T_{n+k}=T_{k+1}T_{n+1}+\left(T_{k}+T_{k-1}\right)T_{n}+T_{k}T_{n-1}.}

The sequence is related to sums of [binomial coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binomial_coefficient) by      T  n + 2   =  ∑  i = 0   ⌊ n  /  2 ⌋    ∑  j = 0   i      (   i j   )       (    n − i − j  i   )      {\displaystyle T_{n+2}=\sum _{i=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor }\sum _{j=0}^{i}{i \choose j}{n-i-j \choose i}}

## Tribonacci spiral

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Tribonacci_ratio&action=edit&section=4)] [![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c3/Tribonacci_spiral.svg/500px-Tribonacci_spiral.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Tribonacci_spiral.svg)Tribonacci spirals with different initial radii on a τ− rectangle.

A tribonacci spiral is a [logarithmic spiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logarithmic_spiral) that gets wider by a factor of ⁠    τ   {\displaystyle \tau }  ⁠ for every quarter turn. It is described by the [polar equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_equation)     r ( θ ) = a exp ⁡ ( k θ ) ,   {\displaystyle r(\theta )=a\exp(k\theta ),}   with initial radius ⁠    a   {\displaystyle a}  ⁠ and parameter     k =   2 π   ln ⁡ ( τ ) .   {\displaystyle k={\frac {2}{\pi }}\ln(\tau ).}

If drawn on a tribonacci rectangle, the spiral has its pole at the foot of altitude of a triangle on the diagonal and passes through vertices of rectangles with aspect ratio ⁠    τ ( τ − 1 )   {\displaystyle \tau (\tau -1)}  ⁠ which are perpendicularly aligned and successively scaled by a factor ⁠     τ  − 1     {\displaystyle \tau ^{-1}}  ⁠. The pole of the spiral divides the main diagonal in ratio ⁠     τ  2     {\displaystyle \tau ^{2}}  ⁠ and [bisects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisection) the diagonal of the lower right rectangle.

In mathematics, the **tribonacci ratio** is a geometrical [proportion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspect_ratio), given by the unique real [solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_root) of the equation *x*3 = *x*2 + *x* + 1. Its decimal expansion begins with 1.839286755214161...  (sequence [A058265](https://oeis.org/A058265) in the [OEIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On-Line_Encyclopedia_of_Integer_Sequences)).

The moniker *tribonacci* was introduced by high-school student Mark Feinberg in an article published in the *[Fibonacci Quarterly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_Quarterly)* of october 1963.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribonacci_ratio#cite_note-2)

Tribonacci sequence

# Tribonacci sequence

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Tribonacci_ratio&action=edit&section=3)]

The first muddled mention of the tribonacci sequence is in [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin)'s *[On the Origin of Species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Origin_of_Species)* (1859), illustrating the population growth of elephants on the supposition that during their lifetime each pair of parents produces three pair of young.[[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribonacci_ratio#cite_note-13)

The number of [compositions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composition_(combinatorics)) of *n* − 2 into parts 1, 2 and 3 is counted by the *n*th tribonacci number (*n* > 1).

The tribonacci sequence is defined by the third-order [recurrence relation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recurrence_relation)      T  n   =  T  n − 1   +  T  n − 2   +  T  n − 3     for   n > 2 ,   {\displaystyle T_{n}=T_{n-1}+T_{n-2}+T_{n-3}{\text{ for }}n>2,}   with initial values      T  0   =  T  1   = 0 ,  T  2   = 1.   {\displaystyle T_{0}=T_{1}=0,T_{2}=1.}

The first few terms are 0, 0, 1, 1, 2, 4, 7, 13, 24, 44, 81, 149, 274, 504, 927,... (sequence [A000073](https://oeis.org/A000073) in the [OEIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On-Line_Encyclopedia_of_Integer_Sequences)).
 The limit ratio between consecutive terms is the tribonacci constant:     lim  n → ∞    T  n + 1    /   T  n   = τ .   {\displaystyle \lim _{n\rightarrow \infty }T_{n+1}/T_{n}=\tau .}
 An alternative relation for ⁠    n > 3   {\displaystyle n>3}  ⁠ is given by      T  n   = 2  T  n − 1   −  T  n − 4   .   {\displaystyle T_{n}=2T_{n-1}-T_{n-4}.}

The sequence is extended to negative indices using      T  n   =  T  n + 3   −  T  n + 2   −  T  n + 1   ,   {\displaystyle T_{n}=T_{n+3}-T_{n+2}-T_{n+1},}   obtaining (0), 1,−1, 0, 2,−3, 1, 4,−8, 5, 7,−20, 18, 9,−47,... (sequence [A057597](https://oeis.org/A057597) in the [OEIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On-Line_Encyclopedia_of_Integer_Sequences)).

The relationship between the negative and positive indexed segments of the sequence is given by      T  1 − n   =  T  n   2   −  T  n + 1    T  n − 1   .   {\displaystyle T_{1-n}=T_{n}^{2}-T_{n+1}T_{n-1}.}   This [reflection formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflection_formula) holds for all integers ⁠    n   {\displaystyle n}  ⁠ and can be derived from Agronomof's identity [[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribonacci_ratio#cite_note-14)[[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribonacci_ratio#cite_note-15)      T  n + k   =  T  k + 1    T  n + 1   +  (   T  k   +  T  k − 1    )   T  n   +  T  k    T  n − 1   .   {\displaystyle T_{n+k}=T_{k+1}T_{n+1}+\left(T_{k}+T_{k-1}\right)T_{n}+T_{k}T_{n-1}.}

The sequence is related to sums of [binomial coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binomial_coefficient) by      T  n + 2   =  ∑  i = 0   ⌊ n  /  2 ⌋    ∑  j = 0   i      (   i j   )       (    n − i − j  i   )      {\displaystyle T_{n+2}=\sum _{i=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor }\sum _{j=0}^{i}{i \choose j}{n-i-j \choose i}}

Viewport width [−3,3], pole zp = 0. The tiny white circle is centred at repelling fixed point zf = τ − 1.

Powers of the tribonacci ratio can be written with tribonacci numbers as quadratic coefficients      τ  n   =  τ  2    T  n   + τ (  T  n − 1   +  T  n − 2   ) +  T  n − 1   ,   {\displaystyle \tau ^{n}=\tau ^{2}T_{n}+\tau (T_{n-1}+T_{n-2})+T_{n-1},}   which is proved by [mathematical induction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_induction) on ⁠    n .   {\displaystyle n.}  ⁠ This relation also holds for ⁠    n < 0.   {\displaystyle n<0.}  ⁠ The order of the coefficients corresponds to the bottom row of matrix ⁠    Q   {\displaystyle Q}  ⁠ below.

The [generating function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generating_function) of the tribonacci sequence for non-negative *n* is given by        x  2    1 − x −  x  2   −  x  3      =  ∑  n = 0   ∞    T  n    x  n     for   x <    1 τ      {\displaystyle {\frac {x^{2}}{1-x-x^{2}-x^{3}}}=\sum _{n=0}^{\infty }T_{n}x^{n}{\text{ for }}x<{\tfrac {1}{\tau }}}

Let ⁠    τ   {\displaystyle \tau }  ⁠ and [complex conjugate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugate_element_(field_theory)) pair ⁠    β   {\displaystyle \beta }  ⁠ and ⁠    γ   {\displaystyle \gamma }  ⁠ be the zeros of polynomial ⁠    f :  x  3   −  x  2   − x − 1   {\displaystyle f:x^{3}-x^{2}-x-1}  ⁠ with [discriminant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discriminant) ⁠    − 44   {\displaystyle -44}  ⁠, the tribonacci numbers are then given by the [Binet formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibonacci_sequence#Binet's_formula)      T  n + 1   = a  τ  n   + b  β  n   + c  γ  n   ,   {\displaystyle T_{n+1}=a\tau ^{n}+b\beta ^{n}+c\gamma ^{n},}   with real ⁠    a   {\displaystyle a}  ⁠ and conjugates ⁠    b   {\displaystyle b}  ⁠ and ⁠    c   {\displaystyle c}  ⁠ the roots of     44  y  3   − 2 y − 1 = 0.   {\displaystyle 44y^{3}-2y-1=0.}

Since      |  b  β  n   + c  γ  n    |  <    2 5      {\displaystyle \left\vert b\beta ^{n}+c\gamma ^{n}\right\vert <{\tfrac {2}{5}}}  , the number ⁠     T  n     {\displaystyle T_{n}}  ⁠ is the [nearest integer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nearest_integer_function) to ⁠     a   τ  n − 1     {\displaystyle \,a\,\tau ^{n-1}}  ⁠, with ⁠    n > 0   {\displaystyle n>0}  ⁠ and coefficient     a =  τ  2    /  (  τ  3   + τ + 2 ) =   {\displaystyle a=\tau ^{2}/(\tau ^{3}+\tau +2)=}   0.3362281169949410942253629... [[a]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribonacci_ratio#cite_note-16)

The tribonacci numbers are obtained as integral powers ⁠    n ≥ 2   {\displaystyle n\geq 2}  ⁠ of [companion matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Companion_matrix) ⁠    Q   {\displaystyle Q}  ⁠ to [characteristic polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristic_polynomial) ⁠    f   {\displaystyle f}  ⁠ with dominant [eigenvalue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eigenvalues_and_eigenvectors#Eigenvalues_and_eigenvectors_of_matrices) ⁠    τ   {\displaystyle \tau }  ⁠ [[16]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribonacci_ratio#cite_note-17)     Q =   (    1   1   1     1   0   0     0   1   0    )     {\displaystyle Q={\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\1&0&0\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix}}}   By the [Cayley–Hamilton theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayley%E2%80%93Hamilton_theorem),      Q  n   =   (     T  n + 2      T  n + 1   +  T  n      T  n + 1        T  n + 1      T  n   +  T  n − 1      T  n        T  n      T  n − 1   +  T  n − 2      T  n − 1      )     {\displaystyle Q^{n}={\begin{pmatrix}T_{n+2}&T_{n+1}+T_{n}&T_{n+1}\\T_{n+1}&T_{n}+T_{n-1}&T_{n}\\T_{n}&T_{n-1}+T_{n-2}&T_{n-1}\end{pmatrix}}}

The [trace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trace_(linear_algebra)#Relationship_to_eigenvalues) of ⁠     Q  n     {\displaystyle Q^{n}}  ⁠ gives the tribonacci-Lucas numbers 3, 1, 3, 7, 11, 21, 39, 71, 131, 241, 443, 815, 1499, 2757,... satisfying the same recurrence relation. Variously,      L  n   = ⌊  τ  n   ⌉   for   n > 3.   {\displaystyle L_{n}=\lfloor \tau ^{n}\rceil {\text{ for }}n>3.}   (sequence [A001644](https://oeis.org/A001644) in the [OEIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On-Line_Encyclopedia_of_Integer_Sequences))

These Lucas numbers have the [Fermat property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat's_little_theorem): if p is prime,      L  p   ≡  L  1    mod  p   .   {\displaystyle L_{p}\equiv L_{1}{\bmod {p}}.}   The converse does not hold, but the small number of tribonacci [pseudoprimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoprime)      n ∣ (  L  n   − 1 )   {\displaystyle \,n\mid (L_{n}-1)}   makes the sequence special. The only composite numbers below 107  to pass the test are n = 182, 25201, 2332, 63618, 194390, 750890, 804055, 1889041, 2487941, 3542533, 3761251, 6829689. (sequence [A371805](https://oeis.org/A371805) in the [OEIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On-Line_Encyclopedia_of_Integer_Sequences))

The tribonacci word sequence on the alphabet ⁠    { a , b , c }   {\displaystyle \{a,b,c\}}  ⁠ is defined by the [substitution rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-Thue_system)       {    a  ↦  a b     b  ↦  a c     c  ↦  a         {\displaystyle {\begin{cases}a\;\mapsto \;ab\\b\;\mapsto \;ac\\c\;\mapsto \;a\end{cases}}}   With initiator ⁠     w  0   = c   {\displaystyle w_{0}=c}  ⁠, the first seven words are     c , a , a b , a b . a c , a b a c . a b a , a b a c a b a . a b a c a b , a b a c a b a a b a c a b . a b a c a b a a b a c   {\displaystyle c,a,ab,ab.ac,abac.aba,abacaba.abacab,abacabaabacab.abacabaabac}   For ⁠    n ≥ 3   {\displaystyle n\geq 3}  ⁠, the series of words ⁠     w  n     {\displaystyle w_{n}}  ⁠ produced by [iterating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iteration) the substitution are similarly obtained by [concatenating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concatenation) the previous three:      w  n   =  w  n − 1   +  w  n − 2   +  w  n − 3   .   {\displaystyle w_{n}=w_{n-1}+w_{n-2}+w_{n-3}.}   The number of *c*, *b* and *a's* in each word is equal to successive tribonacci numbers and the word length ⁠    l (  w  n   ) =  T  n + 2     {\displaystyle l(w_{n})=T_{n+2}}  ⁠.[[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribonacci_ratio#cite_note-18) The tribonacci word sequence is the basis of the construction of the classic [Rauzy fractal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rauzy_fractal).[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribonacci_ratio#cite_note-19)

The [four-number game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ducci_sequence) played with sets ⁠     S  0     {\displaystyle S_{0}}  ⁠ of successive tribonacci numbers ⁠    {  T  n − 3   ,  T  n − 2   ,  T  n − 1   ,  T  n   }   {\displaystyle \{T_{n-3},T_{n-2},T_{n-1},T_{n}\}}  ⁠, and [circular shifts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclic_permutation) or reflections thereof, results in subsequent maximum run lengths     l (  S  0   ) = 3 ⌊     n − 1  2    ⌋ ,   {\displaystyle l(S_{0})=3\lfloor {\tfrac {n-1}{2}}\rfloor ,}   ⁠    n ≥ 3   {\displaystyle n\geq 3}  ⁠. A game of infinite length is obtained by taking real elements      S  0   = { 1 , τ ,  τ  2   ,  τ  3   } ,   {\displaystyle S_{0}=\{1,\tau ,\tau ^{2},\tau ^{3}\},}   given to unlimited precision.[[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribonacci_ratio#cite_note-20)

Tribonacci spiral

# Tribonacci spiral

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Tribonacci_ratio&action=edit&section=4)] [![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c3/Tribonacci_spiral.svg/500px-Tribonacci_spiral.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Tribonacci_spiral.svg)Tribonacci spirals with different initial radii on a τ− rectangle.

A tribonacci spiral is a [logarithmic spiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logarithmic_spiral) that gets wider by a factor of ⁠    τ   {\displaystyle \tau }  ⁠ for every quarter turn. It is described by the [polar equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_equation)     r ( θ ) = a exp ⁡ ( k θ ) ,   {\displaystyle r(\theta )=a\exp(k\theta ),}   with initial radius ⁠    a   {\displaystyle a}  ⁠ and parameter     k =   2 π   ln ⁡ ( τ ) .   {\displaystyle k={\frac {2}{\pi }}\ln(\tau ).}

If drawn on a tribonacci rectangle, the spiral has its pole at the foot of altitude of a triangle on the diagonal and passes through vertices of rectangles with aspect ratio ⁠    τ ( τ − 1 )   {\displaystyle \tau (\tau -1)}  ⁠ which are perpendicularly aligned and successively scaled by a factor ⁠     τ  − 1     {\displaystyle \tau ^{-1}}  ⁠. The pole of the spiral divides the main diagonal in ratio ⁠     τ  2     {\displaystyle \tau ^{2}}  ⁠ and [bisects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisection) the diagonal of the lower right rectangle.
