The **snub decachoron**, or **snad**, also commonly called the **omnisnub pentachoron** or **omnisnub 5-cell**, is a convex [isogonal](https://polytope.miraheze.org/wiki/Isogonal_polytope) [polychoron](https://polytope.miraheze.org/wiki/Polychoron) that consists of 10 [snub tetrahedra](https://polytope.miraheze.org/wiki/Snub_tetrahedron), 20 [triangular gyroprisms](https://polytope.miraheze.org/wiki/Triangular_gyroprism), and 60 [phyllic disphenoids](https://polytope.miraheze.org/wiki/Phyllic_disphenoid). 2 snub tetrahedra, 2 triangular antiprisms, and 4 disphenoids join at each vertex. It can be obtained through the process of [alternating](https://polytope.miraheze.org/wiki/Alternation) the [great prismatodecachoron](https://polytope.miraheze.org/wiki/Great_prismatodecachoron). However, it cannot be made uniform, as it generally has 4 edge lengths, which can be minimized to no fewer than 2 different sizes.

Using the ratio method, the lowest possible ratio between the longest and shortest edges is 1:          3 +   2    3        {\displaystyle {\displaystyle {\sqrt {\frac {3+{\sqrt {2}}}{3}}}}}   ≈ 1:1.21301.

This polychoron generally has a doubled symmetry. A variant without this extended symmetry also exists, known as the [snub pentachoron](https://polytope.miraheze.org/wiki/Snub_pentachoron).

You can help by [adding to them](https://polytope.miraheze.org/wiki/Snub_decachoron?action=edit&section=). *(September 2025)*

A snub decachoron formed directly from alternating a [great prismatodecachoron](https://polytope.miraheze.org/wiki/Great_prismatodecachoron) of edge length 1 has coordinates in 5 dimensions given by all even permutations of:

- (  2   2   ,      3   2    2    ,    2   ,      2  2    ,  0  )  .     {\displaystyle {\displaystyle \left(2{\sqrt {2}},\,{\tfrac {3{\sqrt {2}}}{2}},\,{\sqrt {2}},\,{\tfrac {\sqrt {2}}{2}},\,0\right).}}

An optimized snub decachoron using the absolute value method, where the phyllic disphenoids become [rhombic disphenoids](https://polytope.miraheze.org/wiki/Rhombic_disphenoid), is given by all even permutations of:

- (  0 ,    1 2   ,     3 +   6    6   ,     3 + 2   6    6   ,     3 +   6    3    )  .     {\displaystyle {\displaystyle \left(0,\,{\frac {1}{2}},\,{\frac {3+{\sqrt {6}}}{6}},\,{\frac {3+2{\sqrt {6}}}{6}},\,{\frac {3+{\sqrt {6}}}{3}}\right).}}

Finally, a variant optimized by the ratio method is given by all even permutations of:

- (  0 ,    1 2   ,     2 +   2    4   ,     1 +   2    2   ,     2 +   2    2    )  .     {\displaystyle {\displaystyle \left(0,\,{\frac {1}{2}},\,{\frac {2+{\sqrt {2}}}{4}},\,{\frac {1+{\sqrt {2}}}{2}},\,{\frac {2+{\sqrt {2}}}{2}}\right).}}

The **snub decachoron**, or **snad**, also commonly called the **omnisnub pentachoron** or **omnisnub 5-cell**, is a convex [isogonal](https://polytope.miraheze.org/wiki/Isogonal_polytope) [polychoron](https://polytope.miraheze.org/wiki/Polychoron) that consists of 10 [snub tetrahedra](https://polytope.miraheze.org/wiki/Snub_tetrahedron), 20 [triangular gyroprisms](https://polytope.miraheze.org/wiki/Triangular_gyroprism), and 60 [phyllic disphenoids](https://polytope.miraheze.org/wiki/Phyllic_disphenoid). 2 snub tetrahedra, 2 triangular antiprisms, and 4 disphenoids join at each vertex. It can be obtained through the process of [alternating](https://polytope.miraheze.org/wiki/Alternation) the [great prismatodecachoron](https://polytope.miraheze.org/wiki/Great_prismatodecachoron). However, it cannot be made uniform, as it generally has 4 edge lengths, which can be minimized to no fewer than 2 different sizes.

Using the ratio method, the lowest possible ratio between the longest and shortest edges is 1:          3 +   2    3        {\displaystyle {\displaystyle {\sqrt {\frac {3+{\sqrt {2}}}{3}}}}}   ≈ 1:1.21301.

This polychoron generally has a doubled symmetry. A variant without this extended symmetry also exists, known as the [snub pentachoron](https://polytope.miraheze.org/wiki/Snub_pentachoron).

A snub decachoron formed directly from alternating a [great prismatodecachoron](https://polytope.miraheze.org/wiki/Great_prismatodecachoron) of edge length 1 has coordinates in 5 dimensions given by all even permutations of:

- (  2   2   ,      3   2    2    ,    2   ,      2  2    ,  0  )  .     {\displaystyle {\displaystyle \left(2{\sqrt {2}},\,{\tfrac {3{\sqrt {2}}}{2}},\,{\sqrt {2}},\,{\tfrac {\sqrt {2}}{2}},\,0\right).}}

An optimized snub decachoron using the absolute value method, where the phyllic disphenoids become [rhombic disphenoids](https://polytope.miraheze.org/wiki/Rhombic_disphenoid), is given by all even permutations of:

- (  0 ,    1 2   ,     3 +   6    6   ,     3 + 2   6    6   ,     3 +   6    3    )  .     {\displaystyle {\displaystyle \left(0,\,{\frac {1}{2}},\,{\frac {3+{\sqrt {6}}}{6}},\,{\frac {3+2{\sqrt {6}}}{6}},\,{\frac {3+{\sqrt {6}}}{3}}\right).}}

Finally, a variant optimized by the ratio method is given by all even permutations of:

- (  0 ,    1 2   ,     2 +   2    4   ,     1 +   2    2   ,     2 +   2    2    )  .     {\displaystyle {\displaystyle \left(0,\,{\frac {1}{2}},\,{\frac {2+{\sqrt {2}}}{4}},\,{\frac {1+{\sqrt {2}}}{2}},\,{\frac {2+{\sqrt {2}}}{2}}\right).}}
