In mathematics, the **supersilver ratio** is a geometrical [proportion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspect_ratio), given by the unique real [solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_root) of the equation *x*3 = 2*x*2 + 1. Its decimal expansion begins with 2.2055694304005903...  (sequence [A356035](https://oeis.org/A356035) in the [OEIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On-Line_Encyclopedia_of_Integer_Sequences)).

The name *supersilver ratio* is by analogy with the [silver ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_ratio), the positive solution of the equation *x*2 = 2*x* + 1, and the [supergolden ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supergolden_ratio).

In mathematics, the **supersilver ratio** is a geometrical [proportion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspect_ratio), given by the unique real [solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial_root) of the equation *x*3 = 2*x*2 + 1. Its decimal expansion begins with 2.2055694304005903...  (sequence [A356035](https://oeis.org/A356035) in the [OEIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On-Line_Encyclopedia_of_Integer_Sequences)).

The name *supersilver ratio* is by analogy with the [silver ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_ratio), the positive solution of the equation *x*2 = 2*x* + 1, and the [supergolden ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supergolden_ratio).

# Supersilver rectangle

# Supersilver spiral

[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Supersilver_ratio&action=edit&section=6)] [![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/Supersilver_spiral.svg/500px-Supersilver_spiral.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Supersilver_spiral.svg)Supersilver spirals with different initial angles on a ς− rectangle.

A supersilver spiral is a [logarithmic spiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logarithmic_spiral) that gets wider by a factor of ⁠    ς   {\displaystyle \varsigma }  ⁠ for every quarter turn. It is described by the [polar equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_equation)     r ( θ ) = a exp ⁡ ( k θ ) ,   {\displaystyle r(\theta )=a\exp(k\theta ),}   with initial radius ⁠    a   {\displaystyle a}  ⁠ and parameter     k =   2 π   ln ⁡ ( ς ) .   {\displaystyle k={\frac {2}{\pi }}\ln(\varsigma ).}   If drawn on a supersilver rectangle, the spiral has its pole at the foot of altitude of a triangle on the diagonal and passes through vertices of rectangles with aspect ratio     ς ( ς − 1 )   {\displaystyle \varsigma (\varsigma -1)}   which are perpendicularly aligned and successively scaled by a factor ⁠     ς  − 1     {\displaystyle \varsigma ^{-1}}  ⁠. The pole of the spiral divides the diagonal of the lower right rectangle in ratio ⁠    ς   {\displaystyle \varsigma }  ⁠.
